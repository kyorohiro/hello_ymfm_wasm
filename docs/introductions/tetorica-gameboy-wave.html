What happens in the chip?

Pack 32 four-bit values, two per byte, into the 16-byte waveform RAM at FF30–FF3F. stopAndSetWaveform() stops the Wave DAC before transfer. setNote() maps to NR33 / NR34, setLevel() maps to NR32, and keyOn() enables the DAC and triggers playback.

Changing the pitch from A3 to A4 approximately doubles the read speed of the same 32 points. It does not specify the original pitch of recorded PCM; this example contains no long recorded sample.

Playing the two-cycle waveform at A3 reads the full 32 points at the same speed, but two peaks occur per table traversal. It sounds about an octave higher than a single-cycle waveform. The periodic structure inside the array also affects pitch.

Output level has four choices. The 1/2 and 1/4 options shift four-bit values, unlike a precise continuous gain, and also change small-value steps. Zero mutes through NR32, which differs from stopping the DAC with keyOff(). Even if the plot retains a constant value, an output with no alternating component does not produce a pitched sound.

Pasting a waveform alone may not fully reproduce the original game sound. Pitch, level, trigger timing, and register changes after triggering also shape it. No game-derived waveforms are bundled here.
